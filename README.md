# Johnny Sosa
Head of People at [Cresteo](https://cresteo.com) · Psychologist with an engineering background · Canelones, Uruguay

I lead People at a technology company that builds teams of LATAM engineers for US companies. Most of my work sits between two questions: how do we find, grow and keep great engineers, and how can AI make that work better, not just faster?

## What I lead
- **People strategy** for a ~90-person, fully remote company: hiring, onboarding, development, performance conversations, talent pool and exits.
- **AI adoption across the company:** an AI fluency assessment, an agentic-mindset framework defined with leadership, an internal bootcamp, training paths and partner certifications.
- **Organizational health without survey theater:** quarterly 1:1 conversations, team climate signals and a talent and mood report for leadership.
- **Processes people actually adopt:** contracts, offboarding, compliance and documentation, kept lightweight.

## What I build
I build most of the tools I work with, mainly with Claude Code:
- **A personal context system** (markdown vault, slash commands, validators) that runs my day: morning brief, end-of-day wrap-up, open loops and decisions, with read-only agents gathering signals from Calendar, Gmail, Slack and Notion.
- **Hiring automation on the Greenhouse API:** interview-prep briefs, applicant triage by location and fit, fake-profile detection, scorecards from meeting transcripts and agent-assisted sourcing.
- **Candidate CVs** rendered to the company format with headless Chrome.
- **An onboarding welcome-video pipeline:** Google Forms → Apps Script → GitHub Actions + Remotion → Slack.
- **Local meeting transcription, a weekly market-intelligence digest,** and a coaching loop that reviews my own week.
- **People data:** a single source of truth for people metrics, feeding leadership dashboards.

## How I think about it
- Closeness over process: if you're close to people, you already know what a survey would tell you.
- Structure should enable, not distance.
- AI amplifies judgment; it doesn't replace it.
